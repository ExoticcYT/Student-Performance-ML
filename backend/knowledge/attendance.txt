Attendance and Academic Performance

Regular attendance can give students more opportunities to learn course material, participate in class, ask questions, and receive guidance from instructors. Classroom instruction may include explanations, examples, discussions, demonstrations, and activities that are difficult to fully reproduce outside of class.

Missing classes can result in missed explanations, assignments, announcements, discussions, or opportunities to receive feedback. Repeated absences may make it harder for a student to keep up with the course, particularly when later topics depend on material covered earlier.

Students who need to miss class should make a reasonable effort to obtain the material they missed. Depending on the course, this may involve reviewing lecture notes, course materials, recordings, assignments, or communicating with the instructor or classmates. Students should also pay attention to their course's specific attendance policies and expectations.

Attendance should not be viewed as the only factor determining academic success. A student can attend every class and still struggle if they are not studying effectively or do not understand the material. Similarly, a student who misses some classes can still perform well if they effectively make up the missed work and understand the material.

Students can improve the value of attending class by actively participating rather than simply being physically present. Taking organized notes, asking questions, identifying confusing concepts, and reviewing class material afterward can help reinforce what was learned.

Attendance is therefore best considered alongside other academic factors such as study habits, preparation, motivation, previous performance, and access to academic support. A prediction or recommendation involving attendance should not be interpreted as proof that changing attendance alone will produce a specific change in academic performance.
